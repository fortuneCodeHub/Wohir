# PART 1: THE RULEBOOK

## The core truth behind every rule

When 300 people apply for one role, the recruiter does not read 300 CVs. Before anyone decides if Fortune is good enough, the CV has to survive three rounds:

1. FOUND: the recruiter searches the system for the job title and the 3 or 4 things the hiring manager cannot live without.
2. UNDERSTOOD: the recruiter opens the CV and spends about 30 seconds on titles, companies, and the first line under each role.
3. FORWARDED: the recruiter does not make the final decision. They have to sell the candidate to the hiring manager, usually in one sentence. Round 3 is where most good candidates disappear, and almost nobody knows it exists.

Most people write their CV to be read. This CV is written to get through all 3 rounds.

## Round 1 rules: FOUND

The recruiter searches for the job title plus the 3 or 4 must-have skills. If those words are not on the CV, Fortune does not come up in the search. It does not matter how good he is.

RULE 1.1: Before writing anything, extract from the job ad: the exact job title, and the 3 to 5 non-negotiable requirements (skills, tools, frameworks, domains, years of experience).

RULE 1.2: Use the EXACT words from the job ad. Never paraphrase a keyword. If the ad says "stakeholder management," write "stakeholder management," not "working with different departments." If the ad says "Node.js," write "Node.js," not "Node." If the ad says "RESTful APIs," write "RESTful APIs," not "REST endpoints."

RULE 1.3: The exact job title from the ad must appear on the CV, in the headline directly under Fortune's name, as long as it truthfully describes his work. Never claim a title the portfolio does not support.

RULE 1.4: Every must-have keyword from the ad that Fortune genuinely has must appear at least twice: once in the summary line or skills section, and once inside an experience or project line that proves it.

RULE 1.5: Only use keywords that are true according to Part 2. If the ad requires something Fortune does not have, do not add it. List it in the gap report instead.

RULE 1.6: The skills section must lead with the ad's keywords, in the ad's wording, before listing anything else.

## Round 2 rules: UNDERSTOOD

The recruiter opens the CV and has about 30 seconds. They read job titles, company names, and the first line under each role. If they cannot tell what Fortune actually did, they move on.

RULE 2.1: Under every job and every project, the FIRST line must be the strongest line. Not a duty. A result, a scale, or a concrete thing Fortune owned.

RULE 2.2: Numbers beat descriptions. Use every real number available. The full list of numbers Fortune has earned:
- 900+ tickets sold on GatePass
- 30+ active organizers on GatePass
- 5 AI agents in Phlox
- 5 social platforms in Phlox creator pipeline (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Twitter, Snapchat)
- 24-hour cooling-off period in Phlox payment system
- 4 payment rails in FortunePay (OPay, PalmPay, Kuda, Bank Transfer)
- 6 sports leagues in OnDicts (football, NBA, NFL, MMA, MLB, NHL)
- 3 attempts per minute rate limit on GatePass bot defense
- 100 to 430+ LinkedIn followers in 2 weeks
- 10,000+ weekly LinkedIn impressions at zero spend
- ₦58 billion addressable market for Phlox
- $150,000 SAFE at $1.2M post-money cap for Phlox pre-seed raise
- 7 days to build and ship the AI Email Rewriter
- Thousands of concurrent transactions on the Pi Network bot
- 3 years of experience

RULE 2.3: Never open a role with "Responsible for," "Worked on," "Helped with," "Involved in," or "Duties included." Open with a result verb: Built, Shipped, Stopped, Diagnosed, Automated, Architected, Deployed, Grew, Reversed-engineered, Designed, Scaled.

RULE 2.4: Titles and company names must be instantly readable. Format every entry as: Title, Company or Project, dates, then the strongest line immediately below.

RULE 2.5: Maximum 3 to 4 bullets per role or project. Each bullet must earn its place against the job ad. Cut anything that does not help pass Round 1 or Round 3 for this specific role.

RULE 2.6: Order entries by relevance to the job ad, not by what Fortune is proudest of. The most relevant work goes first.

RULE 2.7: One page if possible, two pages maximum. No graphics, no tables, no columns, no icons, no photos. Plain text that any ATS can parse.

## Round 3 rules: FORWARDED

The recruiter has to sell Fortune to the hiring manager in one sentence. If the recruiter cannot build that sentence from the CV in a few seconds, Fortune does not get forwarded.

RULE 3.1: Write that sentence for the recruiter and put it at the very TOP of the CV, directly under the name and headline.

RULE 3.2: The forwarding sentence formula: [Job title from the ad], [years of experience], [domain or industry that matches the ad], [2 to 3 must-have tools from the ad], [one hard proof point with a number].
Example: "Full-Stack Developer, 3 years building production fintech and payment systems, works in NestJS, TypeScript and PostgreSQL, built an escrow payment system with double-entry ledger and daily reconciliation."

RULE 3.3: The forwarding sentence must be copy-pasteable. A recruiter should be able to lift it word for word into an email to the hiring manager without editing it.

RULE 3.4: The forwarding sentence must use the ad's exact title and exact keywords.

RULE 3.5: Make it easy for the recruiter to sell Fortune. Every section below the forwarding sentence exists only to prove that sentence is true.

## Writing quality rules

RULE W.1: Every project description leads with the problem it solved or the outcome it delivered, not the technology used. Technology is evidence. Outcome is the claim.

RULE W.2: When describing automation or bot projects, name the specific technical mechanism, not the category. "TLS fingerprint profiling, canvas and WebGL fingerprint spoofing, WebRTC leak prevention, residential proxy rotation" is stronger than "anti-detection techniques."

RULE W.3: When describing payment or fintech systems, name the specific safety mechanism and why it matters. "Idempotency keys on every mutation to prevent double-spend" is stronger than "idempotency."

RULE W.4: When describing AI projects, name the specific agent behavior, not just "AI-powered." "Five AI agents: creator sourcing, contract generation, content review against brief, competitor conflict detection, and per-brand learning" is stronger than "AI agents for platform automation."

RULE W.5: When describing security work, lead with the threat that was stopped, then the mechanism. "Stopped a live bot registration attack burning through Brevo's email quota before real users could receive confirmations" is stronger than "implemented rate limiting."

RULE W.6: When describing reverse-engineering work, name what was absent. "Pi Network has no public API documentation. Reverse-engineered the node transaction protocol and authentication handshake from network traffic analysis alone" tells the full story.

RULE W.7: Use active past-tense verbs. Built, Shipped, Stopped, Diagnosed, Automated, Architected, Deployed, Grew, Reversed-engineered, Designed, Scaled, Turned, Fired, Injected, Hooked. Never: was responsible for, was involved in, helped to.

## Universal rules

RULE U.1: No em dashes anywhere in the CV. Use commas, colons, or full stops.
RULE U.2: No invented facts, metrics, employers, titles, or dates. Everything comes from Part 2.
RULE U.3: Location on every CV is Lagos State, Nigeria.
RULE U.4: No buzzword filler ("passionate," "hardworking," "team player," "go-getter," "results-driven") unless the ad uses it as a required keyword.
RULE U.5: Plain text output, ready to paste into a document.
RULE U.6: All projects in Part 2 are always available to draw from. Selection is driven by relevance to the job ad. No project is ever removed from this file.
